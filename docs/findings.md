# What was found in MuJoCo

MuJoCo 3.16.0 as released, its options at their defaults unless a line names one, held to textbook physics and to the
published models it rests on ([the references](references.md)). Every number is from a run in
[`validation/`](../validation/), read by the Senua AI Robotics Core's measuring programs. Design probes find where to
look; a pre-registered study decides. The table says which.

Several of these are the same departures MuJoCo's users report: [the issues](issues.md) counts each once.

## What MuJoCo gets right

- **Deterministic to the byte**: the whole record, played twice, gives the same bytes ([the audit](audit.md)); macOS and
  Linux releases on two Intel processors give the same bytes (run 002); a build from source with gcc 13.3 plays exactly
  as the Linux release, built with clang 20.1.8 (run 004).
- **Friction at an impact**: Coulomb's to 1 in 10,000 at every step (runs 001, 008).
- **Free joint motion**: a free spin keeps its speed, a torque gives torque over inertia exactly, a pendulum keeps its
  swing (run 001).
- **Joint limits** push and never pull (run 001).
- **Small rotations**: every one of the first deep dive's 40 registered rotations is exact; the axis is lost only below
  2e-15 rad, a million times below SuperDex's threshold (run 010).
- **The contact suites**: 9 of the first deep dive's 10 known-answer tests as released, 10 of 10 with the elliptic cone
  and noslip, which also holds at rest and slides the same along every direction played at no cost in solver iterations; two cubes
  keep their momentum (runs 008, 009, 014).

What users report, item by item with its source, is in [known issues](known-issues.md).

## F4 traced: why the default integrator adds energy to a chain

A source trace, 15 October 2026; no new plays. Every number is from runs 001, 007 and 014.

**Where the error enters.** MuJoCo's `Euler` step (`src/engine/engine_forward.c` of 3.16.0, `mj_EulerSkip` and
`mj_advance`) is semi-implicit: it sets the velocity from the acceleration at the step's start, then moves the positions
with the new velocity. That acceleration carries the Coriolis and centripetal force, which `mj_rne` computes once, from
the step's start, as `qfrc_bias` (lines 225 to 226). For a chain the force is quadratic in the joint speeds and the
mass matrix changes with the angles, so each step's change of energy and of angular momentum about the first joint
misses by a term of order h squared. Over a fixed time that is order h: the first-order law run 007 measured (9.72%,
2.48%, 0.61%, 0.15% at 10 ms over 1, 4, 16, 64). One joint has no such force, and run 001's free spin keeps its speed
exactly under the same step.

**How the other integrators differ, as their source says.** `implicitfast` builds its implicit matrix from
`mjd_smooth_vel` with `flg_bias = 0`, leaving the Coriolis and centripetal derivatives out for a jointed chain, so on
this chain it is byte-identical to `Euler` (run 007). `implicit` keeps them (`flg_bias = 1`): the force is taken to
first order at the step's end, and the chain loses energy at about the rate `Euler` gains it (-7.63% at 10 ms) and
blows up when swinging at 10 ms (run 007). `RK4` is fourth order: the free chain keeps its energy and momentum to
1 ppm (run 007), but contact refinement is out of order under it (run 014).

**What MuJoCo's documentation says.** Its computation chapter states that integrating gyroscopic forces explicitly
"can lead to energy gain and divergence", reinstates their derivatives in `implicitfast` only for standalone free
bodies, and names `RK4` as "best for systems which are energy conserving", citing a pendulum mechanism that diverges
under `Euler` and `implicitfast` and loses energy under `implicit`. The record's measurement agrees with each of these
statements on the free two-link chain.

**Whether a correction exists, grounded and with no fitted constant: none was found for a patch.** The literature on
integrating multibody systems gives three remedies, and none is a change to the existing step that is off unless set:

- **A higher-order method.** That is `RK4`, which MuJoCo already has; its cost with contact is measured (run 014).
- **A variational integrator** (Marsden and West, "Discrete mechanics and variational integrators", Acta Numerica,
  2001; for multibody systems in generalised coordinates, Johnson and Murphey, IEEE Transactions on Robotics, 2009).
  It keeps momentum exactly and energy without drift, but each step solves the discrete equations of motion by
  Newton iteration. That is a new integrator, not a correction of `Euler`'s one-step update.
- **Projection onto the conserved quantities** ([Hairer, Lubich and Wanner, Geometric Numerical Integration](references.md#hairer-gni), chapter
  IV.4). It is exact here, but only where energy and momentum are known to be conserved. With contact, actuators,
  damping or gravity doing work they are not, and the projection would change the motion wrongly, so it cannot be an
  engine patch.

A shortcut was also considered and not designed: taking half the Coriolis derivative, between `Euler`'s none and
`implicit`'s all. That is the trapezoidal rule in the velocity alone, while MuJoCo moves positions with the new
velocity, a first-order rule; no cited method combines the two, and it would be a constant of this record's choosing.

**The finding.** F4's cause is the explicit Coriolis and centripetal force of the semi-implicit step, as MuJoCo's own
documentation describes. MuJoCo's remedy is `RK4`. A variational integrator is the grounded way to keep both the
chain's invariants and a one-step contact solve, and it is a new integrator, recorded as open.
